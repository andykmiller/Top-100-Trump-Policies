What is this website?

This website ranks the 100 most consequential Trump policies and actions from 2017–2026 using a published, impact-based methodology. Start with #1 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA), then browse the complete ranking from the home page.

Is this a list of Trump’s accomplishments?

No. It is a ranking of consequences, not praise or criticism. Some readers may regard #1 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA) or #16 Abraham Accords as accomplishments, while others may emphasize their costs or limitations.

Is this website pro-Trump or anti-Trump?

Neither is the intended editorial position. Every major entry presents supportive and critical analysis, cites sources, and explains its rank so readers can reach their own conclusions.

How were the Trump policy rankings determined?

The ranking weighs people affected, economic magnitude, institutional or legal permanence, duration, international impact, and historical significance. See the full methodology.

Can the Trump policy rankings change?

Yes. RM-012 requires reconsideration when implementation, court rulings, economic evidence, or major events change an action’s estimated impact. The addition of #21 U.S. Strikes on Iranian Nuclear Sites and 2025–2026 Iran Escalation is one example.

What were Trump’s biggest accomplishments?

That depends on the reader’s values, but the highest-ranked actions include #1 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA), #2 Supreme Court Appointments, #5 COVID-19 Economic Relief Measures, #6 Operation Warp Speed, and #12 USMCA Trade Agreement. Each entry includes both supportive and critical interpretations.

What were Trump’s biggest failures?

The project does not impose a single success-or-failure judgment. Readers can examine controversies such as #35 Family Separation and Zero Tolerance, #85 2020 Election and January 6-Related Institutional Effects, and #100 Puerto Rico Disaster Response and Funding Disputes and compare the evidence presented in each entry.

What was Trump’s most consequential policy?

Under this methodology, #1 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA) ranks first because of its broad reach, economic magnitude, statutory durability, and long-term fiscal consequences.

What did Trump actually do as president?

The ranking focuses on implemented actions rather than campaign promises alone. Examples include signing #1 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA), appointing the justices covered in #2 Supreme Court Appointments, implementing #3 Immigration Enforcement and Border Security Policies, and creating #20 Space Force Creation.

What were Trump’s major tax policies?

The central tax measure was #1 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA). Its provisions changed individual taxes, corporate taxes, international taxation, deductions, and the federal revenue outlook.
