#76 Police Reform Executive Order Criminal Justice / Policing / Civil Rights63/100

Overview

What Happened

In June 2020, following the murder of George Floyd and nationwide protests, President Trump issued an executive order on policing. The order encouraged law-enforcement agencies to pursue independent credentialing, restricted chokeholds except where deadly force is authorized, promoted information sharing about officers with misconduct records, and directed federal agencies to support training on de-escalation, use of force, and community engagement.

Why It Happened

The administration framed the order as a way to improve policing while rejecting calls to defund or sharply reduce police departments. It sought to acknowledge public concern over misconduct without accepting the broader progressive critique that American policing required structural transformation. The order also fit Trump's emphasis on law and order during the 2020 campaign.

Immediate Effects

Immediate effects were more symbolic and administrative than sweeping. Federal grants and credentialing incentives could encourage some departments to change policies, but policing in the United States is mostly controlled by state and local governments. The order did not create a national use-of-force law, eliminate qualified immunity, or impose mandatory federal standards on all police departments.

Long-Term Consequences

The long-term significance is that the order marked the administration's main federal response to one of the largest protest movements in modern U.S. history. It also exposed the limits of executive action in policing: presidents can influence federal incentives and rhetoric, but Congress, states, cities, unions, courts, and local departments control many of the most consequential rules.

Impact Score 63/100
Population Affected

Police officers, local departments, protesters, communities affected by policing, civil-rights groups, federal grant recipients, and state and local governments.

Economic Magnitude

Moderate direct federal spending implications, but large social and institutional stakes because policing affects public safety, civil rights, lawsuits, municipal budgets, and community trust.

Supportive Analysis

Supporters argued that the order offered practical reform without undermining police capacity during a period of rising public disorder and intense pressure on departments.

They viewed credentialing, better databases, and de-escalation training as targeted ways to reduce misconduct while preserving local control and officer discretion.

The strongest supportive argument is that the order tried to improve accountability without adopting policies supporters believed would weaken public safety or demoralize police.

Critical Analysis

Critics argued that the order was too narrow for the scale of the problem and relied heavily on voluntary compliance rather than enforceable national standards.

Civil-rights advocates said it did not address qualified immunity, police-union contracts, racial profiling, militarization, or the federal role in pattern-or-practice investigations.

Some critics viewed it as a political response designed to avoid deeper reforms while presenting the administration as responsive to public anger.

#77 Civil Unrest and Federal Law Enforcement Deployment Federal Law Enforcement / Civil Unrest / Federalism63/100

Overview

What Happened

During 2020 protests and unrest, the Trump administration used federal law-enforcement personnel to protect federal property, respond to demonstrations, and confront what it described as violent disorder. Deployments involving DHS, U.S. Marshals, Park Police, and other federal personnel became especially controversial in Portland, Oregon, and Washington, D.C.

Why It Happened

The administration argued that local officials were failing to protect federal buildings, officers, and public order. The deployments fit Trump's broader law-and-order message and his contention that federal authority was necessary when cities did not control riots, vandalism, or attacks on courthouses and monuments.

Immediate Effects

Immediate effects included confrontations with protesters, litigation, congressional inquiries, and national debate over federalism. Images of federal officers in tactical gear, use of tear gas or less-lethal munitions, and detentions near protest sites became defining symbols of the administration's response.

Long-Term Consequences

Long-term, the episode raised durable questions about the use of federal force in domestic unrest. It also became a precedent cited in debates over when presidents may use federal officers, how clearly they must be identified, and whether deployment decisions can escalate rather than calm local conflict.

Impact Score 63/100
Population Affected

Protesters, local residents, federal law-enforcement officers, city officials, federal agencies, civil-liberties groups, and communities affected by unrest.

Economic Magnitude

Direct fiscal costs were limited compared with major programs, but municipal damage, policing costs, lawsuits, and political effects were substantial.

Supportive Analysis

Supporters argued that the federal government has a responsibility to protect courthouses, monuments, and federal officers when local officials cannot or will not do so.

They said sustained attacks on federal property are not ordinary protest and that visible federal action can deter violence and restore order.

The strongest supportive argument framed the deployments as defense of lawful federal authority during a period of exceptional unrest.

Sources

  • U.S. Marshals Service agency
  • Federal Protective Service agency
  • White House Archive: Law and Order Statements presidential_archive

Critical Analysis

Critics argued that federal deployments escalated tensions, blurred lines between civil protest and domestic security operations, and infringed on First Amendment activity.

They objected to aggressive tactics, unclear identification, and federal intervention over local objections, arguing that the approach undermined local governance.

Civil-liberties groups warned that normalizing federal protest deployments could chill dissent and expand presidential power in domestic politics.

#78 Antifa Designation Efforts Domestic Security / Political Violence / Civil Liberties62/100

Overview

What Happened

During protests and unrest, President Trump and senior officials repeatedly described antifa as a major source of violence and announced interest in designating it as a terrorist organization. Because U.S. law does not provide the same domestic terrorist organization designation mechanism used for foreign groups, the effort became more significant as rhetoric and enforcement posture than as a durable legal classification.

Why It Happened

The administration argued that organized far-left violence, black-bloc tactics, attacks on police, and property destruction required a stronger federal response. Supporters believed antifa networks exploited lawful protests and that federal officials should treat political violence consistently regardless of ideology.

Immediate Effects

Immediate effects included investigative attention, public messaging, and pressure on law enforcement to distinguish peaceful protesters from violent actors. The proposal also collided with civil-liberties concerns because antifa is decentralized, ideologically broad, and not a membership organization in the way federal terrorist designations typically require.

Long-Term Consequences

The long-term significance is that the episode foreshadowed later disputes over how the United States defines domestic extremism. It also showed the limits of counterterrorism tools when political violence is domestic, loosely organized, and intertwined with protected speech and protest activity.

Impact Score 62/100
Population Affected

Protesters, law-enforcement agencies, political activists, civil-liberties organizations, local governments, and communities affected by protest-related violence.

Economic Magnitude

Direct fiscal impact was limited; the larger stakes involved law-enforcement priorities, litigation risk, civil liberties, and public trust.

Supportive Analysis

Supporters argued that political violence should not be excused because it occurs alongside protests or is committed by decentralized groups.

They said federal attention was necessary where local officials were reluctant to confront groups using intimidation, arson, or assaults against police.

The strongest supportive case was that domestic security policy must address violent extremism across ideologies, including far-left violence.

Sources

  • White House Archive presidential_archive
  • Department of Justice agency
  • FBI: Domestic Terrorism agency
  • Heritage Foundation: Domestic Extremism policy_analysis

Critical Analysis

Critics argued that the proposal was legally confused because domestic groups cannot be designated like foreign terrorist organizations under existing law.

They warned that vague use of the antifa label could sweep in lawful protest, journalism, mutual-aid activity, or ideological association protected by the First Amendment.

Civil-liberties critics also said focusing on antifa risked politicizing domestic security while underplaying other extremist threats.

#79 Social Media Executive Order / Section 230 Technology / Speech / Platform Regulation62/100

Overview

What Happened

In 2020, President Trump issued an executive order targeting online platform moderation after social-media companies added labels or enforcement actions to some of his posts. The order directed agencies to examine whether platforms should lose liability protections under Section 230 when they engaged in moderation the administration viewed as politically biased.

Why It Happened

The administration argued that dominant platforms had become gatekeepers of public discourse while claiming legal protections designed for neutral intermediaries. Supporters said companies should not be able to suppress political viewpoints while enjoying broad immunity from liability for user content.

Immediate Effects

Immediate effects included regulatory petitions, agency reviews, lawsuits, and intensified public debate over whether technology companies censor conservative speech. The order did not rewrite Section 230 by itself, because the statute is controlled by Congress and courts, but it moved platform regulation into the center of national politics.

Long-Term Consequences

Long-term significance is high because the conflict over online speech, misinformation, moderation, and platform power has only grown. The Trump order helped accelerate a bipartisan but divided debate: conservatives often focused on viewpoint discrimination, while liberals often focused on misinformation, hate speech, and platform accountability.

Impact Score 62/100
Population Affected

Social-media users, technology companies, publishers, political campaigns, advertisers, journalists, regulators, and anyone whose speech or business depends on major platforms.

Economic Magnitude

Large indirect economic stakes because Section 230 underpins platform business models, content moderation systems, advertising markets, and online publishing.

Supportive Analysis

Supporters argued that a few dominant platforms exercise enormous influence over political speech and should not receive special immunity while moderating in biased ways.

They said Section 230 was never intended to protect platforms that act like editors while avoiding publisher responsibility.

The strongest supportive argument connected free expression to market power: when online platforms become essential infrastructure, moderation decisions can affect democratic debate.

Sources

  • FCC: Section 230 agency

  • Heritage Foundation: Section 230 policy_analysis

Critical Analysis

Critics argued that the order threatened private companies' First Amendment rights to moderate their own services.

Technology-law experts warned that weakening Section 230 could lead platforms to remove more content, host more harmful content, or restrict user posting to reduce legal risk.

Critics also said the order was retaliatory, triggered by presidential anger at fact-checking rather than a neutral regulatory process.

#80 Net Neutrality Repeal Telecommunications / Internet Regulation61/100

Overview

What Happened

In 2017, the Federal Communications Commission voted to repeal the 2015 Open Internet Order, which had classified broadband internet access as a telecommunications service under Title II of the Communications Act. The repeal, called Restoring Internet Freedom by the FCC, removed federal rules against blocking, throttling, and paid prioritization under that framework.

Why It Happened

The FCC majority argued that Title II regulation discouraged broadband investment and imposed utility-style oversight on a dynamic internet market. Supporters said transparency rules and antitrust or consumer-protection law were better tools than common-carrier regulation.

Immediate Effects

Immediate effects were regulatory and political. Internet providers no longer faced the same federal net neutrality obligations, while states, advocacy groups, and later administrations pursued their own rules or reversals. Consumers did not see an immediate, uniform transformation of internet service, but the legal baseline changed.

Long-Term Consequences

The long-term significance is that net neutrality became a recurring regulatory pendulum. The repeal affected investment debates, state authority, federal preemption, and the legal classification of broadband. It remains one of the clearest examples of how FCC composition can reshape internet governance.

Impact Score 61/100
Population Affected

Internet users, broadband providers, streaming services, online businesses, state regulators, schools, rural communities, and digital-rights groups.

Economic Magnitude

Large sectoral stakes involving broadband investment, internet services, content delivery, compliance costs, and platform competition.

Supportive Analysis

Supporters argued that the internet grew successfully before Title II regulation and that heavy-handed rules could reduce incentives for broadband deployment.

They said broadband providers should disclose practices but retain flexibility to manage networks and offer differentiated services.

The strongest supportive case framed repeal as restoring a lighter regulatory model that encourages investment and innovation.

Sources

  • FCC: Restoring Internet Freedom Order agency
  • Federal Register: Restoring Internet Freedom federal_register
  • USTelecom: Broadband Policy industry
  • American Enterprise Institute: Net Neutrality policy_analysis

Critical Analysis

Critics argued that without enforceable rules, broadband providers could favor their own services, disadvantage competitors, or create paid fast lanes.

Digital-rights groups said internet access functions like essential infrastructure and should not depend only on market discipline in areas with limited competition.

Critics also warned that repeal created uncertainty as states and future FCC majorities tried to restore protections.

Sources

  • Electronic Frontier Foundation: Net Neutrality digital_rights
  • Free Press: Net Neutrality advocacy

  • Congressional Research Service: Net Neutrality research

Why This Rank?

Overall

Overview

What Happened

Trump-era telecommunications policy included efforts to expand rural broadband funding, streamline wireless infrastructure deployment, free spectrum for 5G, and accelerate the buildout of next-generation networks. The FCC played a central role through auctions, subsidy programs, and deregulatory actions affecting small cells, pole attachments, and rural providers.

Why It Happened

The administration argued that broadband access is essential for economic opportunity, telemedicine, education, precision agriculture, and national competitiveness. 5G was also framed as a strategic competition with China, especially as disputes over Huawei and network security intensified.

Immediate Effects

Immediate effects included major FCC auctions, rural funding commitments, and regulatory fights with state and local governments over siting authority and fees. The policies did not close the rural digital divide, but they directed federal attention toward broadband as infrastructure rather than luxury service.

Long-Term Consequences

Long-term significance is tied to whether broadband becomes universal infrastructure comparable to electricity or highways. The pandemic later reinforced the importance of reliable internet for work, school, health care, and government services, making the Trump-era broadband and 5G efforts part of a longer policy arc.

Impact Score 61/100
Population Affected

Economic Magnitude

Supportive Analysis

Supporters argued that federal policy needed to accelerate deployment in rural and underserved areas where market incentives alone were insufficient.

They said faster 5G rollout would support innovation, manufacturing, autonomous systems, telemedicine, and competition with China.

The strongest supportive case linked broadband access to basic economic participation in modern life.

#82 Infrastructure Permitting Reforms Infrastructure / Environmental Review / Federal Permitting61/100

Overview

What Happened

This entry captures infrastructure-focused permitting reforms not already covered in the broader federal permitting entry. The administration emphasized roads, bridges, pipelines, water projects, ports, and energy infrastructure, arguing that environmental reviews and multi-agency approvals often delayed construction for years.

Why It Happened

Officials argued that a project can be effectively killed by delay even when it is never formally rejected. The administration promoted a One Federal Decision approach, lead-agency coordination, and tighter review timelines to make approvals more predictable.

Immediate Effects

Immediate effects included new agency procedures, disputes over NEPA scope, litigation, and stronger political attention to permitting as a bottleneck. Developers welcomed predictability, while environmental and community groups warned that speed could come at the expense of scrutiny.

Long-Term Consequences

Long-term consequences include a lasting bipartisan recognition that permitting is central to infrastructure delivery. Later debates over clean energy, transmission lines, mining, and transportation continued to wrestle with the same tension between faster building and public review.

Impact Score 61/100
Population Affected

Project developers, construction workers, transportation agencies, local communities, environmental reviewers, Tribal governments, and infrastructure users.

Economic Magnitude

Potentially large because permitting affects multibillion-dollar infrastructure projects, even though the reforms themselves were procedural.

Supportive Analysis

Supporters argued that environmental review should not take longer than construction and that uncertainty raises costs for public and private projects.

They said coordinated agency review can prevent duplicative paperwork while preserving substantive environmental analysis.

The strongest supportive case is practical: infrastructure policy fails if approved money cannot be turned into completed projects.

#83 Buy American and Federal Procurement Rules Industrial Policy / Federal Procurement / Manufacturing60/100

Overview

What Happened

Trump-era procurement policy emphasized buying American-made goods, reviewing waivers, increasing domestic-content thresholds, and using federal purchasing as a tool of industrial policy. Executive orders directed agencies to favor U.S.-made products and reduce reliance on foreign suppliers where possible.

Why It Happened

The administration argued that federal spending should support American workers, manufacturing capacity, and national security. Buy American policy fit the broader critique of globalization, trade deficits, and supply-chain dependence that shaped tariffs and China policy.

Immediate Effects

Immediate effects included agency reviews, contractor compliance questions, and closer scrutiny of waivers. The policies did not remake federal procurement overnight, because supply chains are complex and many statutory exceptions already exist. Still, they signaled a shift from lowest-cost procurement toward resilience and domestic preference.

Long-Term Consequences

The long-term significance is that Buy American became part of a bipartisan industrial-policy revival. Later administrations expanded domestic-content rules, showing that Trump's procurement nationalism helped move the policy center of gravity toward supply-chain resilience and manufacturing strategy.

Impact Score 60/100
Population Affected

Federal contractors, manufacturers, workers, agencies, infrastructure suppliers, foreign trading partners, and taxpayers.

Economic Magnitude

Large procurement implications because federal purchasing totals hundreds of billions annually, though effects vary by sector and contract.

Supportive Analysis

Supporters argued that taxpayer dollars should strengthen domestic industry instead of subsidizing foreign competitors.

They said procurement rules can protect strategic supply chains and maintain manufacturing capacity that markets alone may offshore.

The strongest supportive case connects procurement to national resilience: government should know where essential goods come from before a crisis.

Overview

What Happened

In 2018, the Trump administration imposed tariffs on imported steel and aluminum using Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act, arguing that reliance on foreign metals threatened national security. The tariffs applied broadly at first, including to allies, though exemptions and quota arrangements later varied by country.

Why It Happened

The administration argued that domestic steel and aluminum capacity is essential for defense, infrastructure, and industrial resilience. Supporters saw the tariffs as a long-overdue response to global overcapacity, especially from China, and to unfair trade practices that had weakened U.S. producers.

Immediate Effects

Immediate effects included higher costs for metal-consuming industries, retaliation from trading partners, negotiations over exemptions, and renewed investment or price support for some domestic producers. Manufacturers using steel and aluminum often objected that tariffs raised input costs and reduced competitiveness.

Long-Term Consequences

The long-term significance is that Section 232 tariffs normalized national-security arguments for economic protection in sectors beyond defense procurement. They also strained relations with allies and blurred the distinction between trade policy and security policy.

Impact Score 60/100
Population Affected

Steel and aluminum workers, manufacturers, construction firms, automakers, consumers, exporters affected by retaliation, trading partners, and defense suppliers.

Economic Magnitude

Supportive Analysis

Supporters argued that steel and aluminum are foundational industries needed for defense readiness, infrastructure, and industrial independence.

They said years of global overcapacity and unfair trade had hollowed out U.S. production and required stronger action than ordinary trade cases.

The strongest supportive argument is that market prices alone do not capture the strategic cost of losing domestic metals capacity.

#85 China Phase One Trade Deal Trade Policy / China / Agriculture59/100

Overview

What Happened

In January 2020, the United States and China signed the Phase One trade agreement after a prolonged tariff fight. China committed to increase purchases of U.S. goods and services, strengthen some intellectual-property protections, reduce certain agricultural barriers, and address financial-services and currency issues. Many tariffs remained in place.

Why It Happened

The administration argued that the deal showed tariffs had forced China to negotiate and commit to measurable purchases. Supporters saw it as a first step toward correcting trade imbalances, protecting intellectual property, and improving market access for farmers and exporters.

Immediate Effects

Immediate effects included temporary market relief, optimism among some agricultural exporters, and a pause in further escalation. The COVID-19 pandemic soon disrupted trade flows, and China did not fully meet purchase targets, which limited the deal's practical effect.

Long-Term Consequences

Long-term significance lies in what the deal did and did not do. It confirmed that U.S.-China trade relations had entered a more confrontational era, but it did not resolve subsidies, state-owned enterprises, industrial policy, technology transfer, or strategic rivalry.

Impact Score 59/100
Population Affected

Farmers, exporters, importers, manufacturers, consumers, financial firms, Chinese companies, trade negotiators, and global supply chains.

Economic Magnitude

Hundreds of billions in targeted purchase commitments and tariff-affected trade flows, though realized purchases fell short.

Supportive Analysis

Supporters argued that the deal extracted commitments from China that previous diplomatic engagement had failed to secure.

They said tariffs created leverage and that purchase commitments offered immediate opportunities for farmers and exporters hurt by retaliation.

The strongest supportive case is that Phase One changed the assumption that China trade disputes should be handled mainly through quiet diplomacy.
